## Assumptions to test before assigning movements

1. A strong explanation can reflect fast damage, sustained defense, or a resource
   investment that paid off. Do not require every winning strategy to maximize
   damage AND block AND speed.
2. Health margin is separate from deck performance. Starting low must not itself
   demote a capable deck; ending low after spending much of the available budget
   is an observed narrow finish, not proof the deck was weak.
3. Setup/HP costs can buy output or control. They are costs to account for, not
   automatic evidence of poor play or cause of victory.
4. A high-output turn does not prove consistent output. Show the observed profile
   before making a consistency claim. Different-draw win probability is unknown.
5. Same scope must be compared: fight to fight, act to act, whole run to whole run.
   One excellent Mid-run boss fight does not reclassify that entire run.
6. Winning alone does not merit promotion. Losing does not mean every capability
   was poor. Old grade distributions intentionally include winning Mid/Rough runs.

## Replacement-grade test, if pursued

Define what each new label means and its measurable thresholds before counting
promotions/demotions. Benchmarks must retain encounter, act, cumulative difficulty,
character/sample coverage and measurement source. Boss identity and damage already
observed may enter retrospective explanations; future state must not enter an
explanation claimed at an earlier checkpoint.

Then produce a transition matrix with unchanged/up/down/unknown counts, preserve
all failures and contradictory examples, and manually inspect each changed
example against its arithmetic and turn profile. Also report coverage and missing
logs by old label, outcome and player. Do not hide missing Rough logs, mix bosses
to manufacture an ordering, or call a partial log a full-run power profile.

Until that rule exists, grade movement is **unassigned**, not 'zero changes' and
not evidence the new framing agrees with the old ranking. The first product
recommendation is richer per-fight/act explanations alongside familiar run labels;
a run-grade redesign is a separate, explicit decision.
